# Case Studies — Where Merchants Already Pay, Read Like a Builder

> Reference format: [shopify.com/case-studies](https://www.shopify.com/tr/case-studies) — 583 stories, each
> one a brand, a before/after and one headline metric. Shopify writes them to sell Shopify. This file
> reads the same stories to answer one question, in Alex Hormozi's order of priorities:
> **how do I make money with this?** Verified: August 2026.

## 0. The only question — and the rule for reading

Every case study below is scored on three things, in this order:

1. **Who paid, and what was it worth to them?** A merchant who lifted AOV from €1,250 to €1,650 on
   ~10,000 orders/year found **€4M/year**. That is the value pool. An app that captures 1–5% of a value
   pool it demonstrably created is *cheap* to the merchant and *rich* to the builder.
2. **Is the outcome repeatable with software, or was it people?** "We hired a great CMO" is not an app.
   "We added a loyalty layer and repeat orders rose 356%" is.
3. **What could a solo builder charge for the piece an app can own?** Hormozi's value equation:
   *(Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort)*. Apps win on the denominator —
   they collapse time and effort. Price on the numerator.

Shopify highlights six outcome categories: migrations, operational efficiency, revenue growth, market
expansion, unified commerce (DTC + POS + B2B) and innovation velocity. Only three of those are
app-shaped for a solo builder: **revenue growth, market expansion, operational efficiency**. Ignore the rest.

## 2. Patterns — what the money says

### Pattern A: the metric the merchant already tracks is the product
Every headline is AOV, repeat rate, conversion, GMV, markets, or hours. Nobody was sold "features".
**Hormozi:** sell the *outcome* the buyer already wants and already measures; the app is the delivery
vehicle. Your listing's first line should be the metric, not the mechanism.

### Pattern B: value pools are enormous relative to app prices
Labellov's AOV lift is worth ~€4M/year. The most expensive app on the App Store is $999/month.
Median entry price is $9.99. **The gap between value created and price charged is the opportunity** —
which is why "charge more" is almost always the right correction. A €300/month app that moves AOV by
1% for a €5M store returns 14× its cost. Say so on the pricing page.

### Pattern C: retention and B2B are where the least competition meets the biggest pools
- Repeat/loyalty (WOW, Original's, BrainGain): 3.5× repeat orders is the single largest multiplier in
  any story — and loyalty apps are fewer and pricier than SEO/popup apps.
- B2B (Vondels, Solberg, MR MARVIS): wholesale merchants have budgets and procurement habits; the App
  Store's B2B category is thin (see [03](03-EKOSISTEM-VERILERI.md) §7b).

### Pattern D: "time back" is a price, not a vibe
Cocunat's "50% more time on innovation" is the ops-automation pitch. Price it on hours:
a founder's hour at €100 × 10 hours/week saved = **€4,000/month of value**. A €200/month app is 5% of that.

### Pattern E: peaks justify annual pricing
JustMyLook's £1M day is the whole year's margin in one weekend. Apps that guarantee uptime, sync and
capacity during peaks can price *annually* and *up*; merchants won't churn a peak-critical tool in
March to save €50.

## 3. Anti-patterns — where the story looks good but the money isn't

| Looks like an opportunity | Why it isn't (for a solo builder) |
|---|---|
| Speed/mobile performance (ARMEDANGELS) | Saturated category; price race to $9.99; Shopify keeps absorbing it into themes |
| Migrations (VIA VAI, Unity Brands) | Agency work, not software; one-off revenue, no LTV |
| Retail/EDI scale (Grüns) | Enterprise sales cycles; needs a team and certifications |
| "Innovation velocity" | Not a product — a consequence of the merchant's team |

## 4. The offer this implies (Hormozi's Grand Slam Offer, applied)

Take Pattern C. A retention/subscription tool for consumable brands in the €1–5M band:

- **Dream outcome:** "Your repeat-order rate up 30% in 90 days, measured in your own analytics."
- **Perceived likelihood:** show the three case studies above with the numbers; offer a 90-day guarantee
  (refund if the metric doesn't move — you'll rarely pay it, and it removes the buying risk).
- **Time delay:** live in one afternoon; first replenishment email fires within 7 days.
- **Effort:** zero-config defaults from order history; the merchant edits copy, not logic.
- **Price:** €149/month + 1% of subscription GMV above €10k/month. Annual: €1,490 (2 months free).
  Anchor against the €4,000/month of founder time it replaces, not against the $9.99 apps.
- **Bonuses:** a merchant-facing dashboard that *proves* the lift (the thing that makes the guarantee safe).

50 merchants × €149 = **€7,450 MRR** — inside the 3–15k/month band a solo builder can actually reach
([03](03-EKOSISTEM-VERILERI.md) §2), with churn structurally low because the tool is tied to revenue.

## 5. What to do with this file (Phase 5 — Problem)

1. Pick **one** pattern (C is the strongest by numbers). Don't build anything.
2. Find 20 merchants that look like WOW Concept / BrainGain (consumables, €1–5M, Storeleads filters).
3. DM the one-sentence outcome: *"Repeat orders +30% in 90 days or you don't pay — 15-minute call?"*
4. Five replies with a real pain = the problem. Zero = next pattern. Either way it cost a day, not a month.

The first priority is not the app. It is proving someone will pay the number above. Everything in this
document exists to make that conversation land.
